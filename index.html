LOCK 1 — 3-DIGIT Beat Frequency
Two sound waves travel through the same medium. Wave A has a frequency of 440 Hz (concert A). Wave B has a speed of 343 m/s and a wavelength of 0.800 m.

(a) Calculate the frequency of Wave B.
(b) Calculate the beat frequency heard when both waves play simultaneously.

The 3-digit code is the beat frequency in Hz.
Use v = fλ to find the frequency of Wave B: f = v ÷ λ = 343 ÷ 0.800.
Beat frequency = |f_A − f_B|. The answer is a whole number between 100–999.
LOCK 2 — 6-LETTER Boundary Behavior — Medium Change
A periodic wave travels from a thin string into a thick string at a boundary. The thick string has a greater linear mass density.

(a) What happens to the wave speed as it enters the thick string?
(b) The frequency remains constant across the boundary. What happens to the wavelength?

The 6-letter code is the one-word answer to what happens to the wave speed when moving from thin string to thick string.

Recall: v = √(T/μ). A greater mass density μ means a _______ wave speed.
v = √(T/μ). Tension T is constant across the boundary. If mass density μ increases, the speed decreases. The wavelength also decreases (λ = v/f, f is constant). The code is the 6-letter word describing what the speed does.
LOCK 3 — 8-LETTER Double-Slit Interference
In Young's double-slit experiment, a stable interference pattern with clearly defined bright and dark fringes is only produced when the two sources of light have a constant phase relationship.

(a) What property must the two light sources have in order to produce a stable interference pattern?
(b) Why does white light from two separate light bulbs NOT produce a stable double-slit pattern?

The 8-letter code is the adjective describing the required property of the light sources.

Starts with C. Think: lasers have this property. Two different light bulbs do NOT.
For a stable interference pattern, waves must have a constant phase difference — they must be coherent. Lasers are coherent. Regular light bulbs emit photons at random phases (incoherent) and don't form a stable pattern.
LOCK 4 — ADDRESS Total Internal Reflection & Snell's Law
Light travels from glass (n = 1.50) into air (n = 1.00).

(a) Using Snell's Law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, calculate the critical angle θ_c for total internal reflection. Round to the nearest whole degree.
(b) What phenomenon occurs when light hits the boundary at exactly θ_c or beyond?

The address lock answer is formatted as:
[critical angle in degrees] [phenomenon name] STREET

e.g. if the angle were 30° and the phenomenon were REFRACTION: "30 REFRACTION STREET" — but that's not the answer.
At the critical angle: n₁ sin θ_c = n₂ sin 90° → sin θ_c = n₂/n₁ = 1.00/1.50 = 0.6667 → θ_c = sin⁻¹(0.6667) ≈ 42°.
When light hits at or beyond the critical angle, it undergoes Total Internal Reflection.
Format: 42 TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION STREET
